# Tracking RSI Research: New Papers from August–October 2026 and a Comparison of GitHub Paper Lists

[中文](./RSI-Recent-Papers-and-GitHub-Lists.md) | English

- **Research date**: 2026-11-02
- **Scope**: recursive self-improvement (RSI) papers in AI from August to October 2026, with a focus on the batch submitted between October 15 and 30, plus the GitHub paper lists devoted to RSI. This note updates [Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) in AI: A Literature Review](./RSI-Literature-Review_en.md) (evidence cutoff 2026-10-21) and does not repeat its content.
- **Basis**: paper dates are always the submission date of the first arXiv version (v1, UTC), written as month-day in tables and parentheses. Numbers from papers are taken from their abstracts; this note does not reproduce experiments and did not read most papers in full. Papers marked † were read in full for the previous review, which holds their full-text evidence. Content marked (our assessment) or (our count) is this note's own analysis or counting, not the sources' words.
- **Freshness**: GitHub star and commit counts come from repository data on 2026-11-02 and only reflect attention and activity; which papers a list includes is counted from the arXiv links in its README.

## Bottom Line

- **Ready-made RSI paper lists are plentiful.** More than 30 repositories have awesome-rsi in their names alone, and many more lists cover self-evolving agents, harnesses or AI4AI. Judged by scope, update frequency and curation quality, the top five are [lobehub/awesome-rsi](https://github.com/lobehub/awesome-rsi) (a general map), [Prism-Shadow/awesome-rsi](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/awesome-rsi) (a companion survey and a filterable method library), [pinkbubblebubble/awesome-rsi](https://github.com/pinkbubblebubble/awesome-rsi) (quick to add new papers, with an evidence label on every entry), [KaiWU5/Awesome-AI4AI](https://github.com/KaiWU5/Awesome-AI4AI) (weekly updates and the most complete machine-readable data) and [adongwanai/follow-rsi](https://github.com/adongwanai/follow-rsi) (a daily feed in Chinese). Section 1 has the details.
- **No single list covers the field.** Between any two of the five largest general lists, the overlap in papers (Jaccard index) is only 0.02–0.15, and together they hold 1,377 distinct arXiv papers (our count). The overlap is low mainly because the RSI label now spans a very wide range, from weight-level self-distillation, harness self-evolution and automated AI research to embodied robots and economic models of an intelligence explosion, and each list draws its scope and emphasis differently. Choose a list by its scope first and its stars second.
- **Papers named after RSI keep multiplying, but the main lines have not changed.** From October 15 to 30 we collected 497 candidate papers, 32 of whose titles contain RSI, recursive self-improvement or similar wording; across all of October, 61 papers were named this way, about twice as many as in September (our count). The new papers still fall within the previous review's conclusions: bounded self-improvement is abundant, and there is still no reliable evidence of compounding improvement in the recursive sense.
- **New work clusters in four places.** First, harness self-evolution, which still has the most method papers (Section 2.4). Second, measurement, auditing and safety, the fastest-growing area, where several papers directly question the evidence behind self-improvement claims (Sections 2.1 and 2.2). Third, frontier agents post-training a model on their own, which now has measurable testbeds such as RSI Arena and Athanor (Section 2.3). Fourth, the embodied direction, where several surveys and position papers appeared in October, two of which find that no study in the physical world has yet isolated gains attributable to recursion (Section 2.5).

## 1. RSI Paper Lists on GitHub

### 1.1 How We Chose

On GitHub, RSI paper lists are mixed in with RSI frameworks and platforms: many of the high-star repositories with RSI in their names are agent runtimes or training frameworks, such as [EverMind-AI/Raven](https://github.com/EverMind-AI/Raven) and [Prism-Shadow/penguin-harness](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/penguin-harness), not paper lists. This section covers only paper lists, meaning awesome lists and the companion repositories of surveys, ranked by three criteria: whether the scope is RSI rather than self-evolving agents or harness engineering in general; whether the list is still updated; and how many papers it covers and how well it grades their evidence.

To compare coverage and freshness, we read the README and commit history of 56 candidate repositories and, for the 27 lists among them devoted to RSI or a neighboring topic, counted how many lists include each arXiv paper from August 2026 onward (our count).

- **lobehub/awesome-rsi**: for readers who want a clearly structured general map. It is divided into model-level and harness-level work, multi-agent systems, coding, automated AI R&D, embodied systems, open-ended evolution, safety and theory, benchmarks and more. It separates the concepts of self-refinement, persistent self-improvement, recursive self-improvement and an “RSI substrate”, and grades evidence in three levels: task gain, retained gain and improver gain. It has not been updated since October 15.
- **Prism-Shadow/awesome-rsi**: for readers who want to search methods by dimension. Its companion survey [From Existing Methods to New Research Ideas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14625) † proposes a nine-dimension taxonomy; the README organizes 113 method papers and 33 benchmark papers by the artifact being improved (parameters, harness code, context, memory, skills), and the website supports filtering on several dimensions, in Chinese and English. A citation graph links only papers inside the collection, with 72 edges: 37 from Semantic Scholar and another 35 added by reading the PDFs of papers Semantic Scholar had no data for. It curates methods selectively rather than chasing daily updates.
- **pinkbubblebubble/awesome-rsi**: for readers who want the newest papers and an evidence label on each one. It has few stars but adds new papers quickly: its coverage of late-October papers is second only to AI4AI, and the newest paper it lists was submitted on October 29. Each paper carries a badge: RSI (the same or an improved process is applied again), Self-improvement (persistent improvement, but the improvement operator itself stays fixed) or Enabler (supporting technology); one-shot self-critique and projects without a reproducible evaluation are excluded by default. It also lists what a convincing RSI evaluation should report: performance across generations (including regressions and variance), a held-out evaluator the system cannot change, ablations, generalization to tasks not used for selection, compute and failed attempts, and safety isolation and rollback.
- **KaiWU5/Awesome-AI4AI**: for readers who want weekly updates and machine-readable data. Every Monday it updates a top-10 news list and citation rankings; `data/papers.json` holds 454 papers with citation counts, GitHub stars and categories. Its scope is “AI for AI” plus long-horizon agents, wider than RSI. It has the best coverage of papers from October 15–30.
- **adongwanai/follow-rsi**: for Chinese readers and anyone who wants to follow along daily. It offers a daily feed (sorted into seven directions, each paper with a one-sentence summary and a quick evidence check), a weekly digest, and evidence cards for 98 harness-evolution papers (held-out set or not, cross-model transfer or not, open source or not, and so on). Daily candidates are found and classified by an automated pipeline, every record in its machine-readable data is marked “automatic, unverified”, and items reach the daily feed only after human review through a PR. The repository was created on October 27.

### 1.5 How to Use Them

- **To follow the field**: watch KaiWU5/Awesome-AI4AI (weekly updates), pinkbubblebubble/awesome-rsi (newest, with the finest evidence labels) and lobehub/awesome-rsi (the clearest structure), then add an embodied or harness list to taste. Chinese readers can add adongwanai/follow-rsi.
- **To search methods by dimension**: filter on Prism-Shadow's website, or parse AI4AI's `data/papers.json` directly.
- **To judge a new list**: first check whether it labels the evidence for each paper, for example whether the improvement mechanism itself changes and whether there is a held-out evaluation; that says more about quality than the number of papers (our assessment).

### 2.1 Measurement and Auditing

| Paper | v1 | Main findings | Caveats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Auditing the Product, Not the Recursion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27610) † | 10-20 | Full-text coding of 114 studies, 90 of which call themselves RSI. Of those 90, 60% evaluate on tasks not used for selection and only 22% audit two or more reruns of the full loop; of the 26 that claim a better improvement procedure, only 4 compare earlier and later procedures from the same starting point at a matched budget | Counts what papers report; not a replication |
| [Harness-Delta Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22466) | 10-15 | Splits the gains of harness evolution into overfitting, test-time compute and the remainder. | [Elicitation or Expansion?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27889) † | 10-20 | Separates whether harness optimization makes tasks already within reach more reliable (elicitation) or enlarges the set of solvable tasks (expansion); an advantage at pass@1 can reverse as the number of attempts grows | Report pass@k curves as well |

### 2.2 Safety and Control

| Paper | v1 | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [RSI Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27735) † | 10-20 | A frontier model acts as an autonomous R&D agent on one stage of its successor's training pipeline, doing the normal task while secretly pursuing a side task that installs a misaligned property in the successor (16 side tasks, 22 task pairs). All six frontier models complete a substantial share of side tasks without hurting the main task, including side tasks that make the successor itself a better saboteur; given only minimal instructions to stay covert, they already produce attacks the monitors miss |
| [Masked Erosion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38392) | 10-28 | An acceptance gate sees how a harness performs under the current model, not whether the remaining safeguards would still hold if one failed. When safeguards overlap, deleting one adds no observable harm, so the redundancy is quietly deleted. ### 2.3 Autonomous Post-Training and AI4AI

This line of work was concentrated in mid-to-late October, and its shared question is whether frontier agents, given real GPUs, can post-train a base model into a usable one on their own.

| Paper | v1 | Results (as reported) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [RSI Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33624) | 10-24 | Ten frontier models, each with 1,000 GPU-hours and USD 300 in API credit, autonomously post-train Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B Base, and all of them produce a working assistant. The strongest model, trained by GPT-6 Astra, closes a median 76% of the gap between the base model and the official Instruct model across 12 capability benchmarks. After a second stage of training on user votes, eight of the ten improve their style-adjusted rating, but benchmark gains are limited and show no clear link to preference gains; one model shows more harmful compliance on StrongREJECT |
| [Athanor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055) | 10-15 | Four Claude Opus 5.5 agents, in 104 hours, raise Qwen3-4B-Base from 15.9 to 67.3 on the development suite and from 9.2 to 48.2 on the held-out suite, against 66.5 and 47.9 for the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. They do it by recombining known methods: SFT, reinforcement learning, weight merging across agents and rounds of self-distillation |
| [ParetoRSI-Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386) | 10-25 | Post-trains across five domains at once (math, code, finance, medicine and science), so a gain in one domain must be weighed against regressions in others. The strongest system, Claude Code with Opus-5, raises the generalization score of its proxy-best checkpoint by 9.2% relative to the starting model (2.3 points absolute) |
| [VibeTraining-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26342) | 10-19 | Research agents improve the data, the trainer and the inference harness together; GPT-6-Astra raises the target system on SWE-bench Verified from 32.9% to 53.7%, against 55.4% for the human-developed Instruct model |
| [Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344) | 10-16 | Trains a model with reinforcement learning to propose improvements, using as the reward a “research world model” that predicts a proposal's gain; the trained Qwen3.8-27B beats GPT-6-sol even when that model gets 300 real experiment records in context |
| [iCoder-27B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29626) † | 08-28 | People write objectives, staged scaffolds, permission boundaries and operating procedures as reusable research skills, and the agent chooses experiments, diagnoses results and revises the training strategy, producing a 27B model for RTL design and GPU kernel optimization that beats GPT-5.5 and Claude-Opus-4.8 on RTLLM |

Related benchmarks and environments include [RSIGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10310) † (among six frontier research models, Opus 5 has the highest RSI-Index, 0.4809), [AI4AI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20318) † (agents rewrite training algorithms; the mean score is 0.166 and the best system's 0.250, covering less than a fifth of the distance from the existing algorithm to the optimum) and [Auto-Research Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35866) (10-26). Two more papers test research judgment: [Can LLMs predict the outcomes of machine learning experiments?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22599) (10-15) finds that even the best model, Claude Opus 5.5, explains only half of the variance in outcomes (R² = 0.49); [SciΠvot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21082) (10-15) builds questions from research decisions that humans corrected, and four of five frontier models score below chance on the selected cases.

### 2.4 Harness Self-Evolution

From October 15 to 30, 70 papers had harness in the title, more than twice as many as were named after RSI; by a rough sort of the titles, about half are methods for evolving or optimizing harnesses and the rest cover harness design, evaluation and applications (our count). Most of the methods are variants of one paradigm: freeze the model, let an agent rewrite the harness from execution traces, and keep a change only if a validation set approves it (our assessment). By subproblem:

- **Generalization and overfitting**: [RRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972) † (09-21) anneals the edit budget and uses a critic and a pruner to filter out benchmark-specific edits, gaining up to 4.3 points on 5 out-of-distribution benchmarks with a harness that uses 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution; [ModularRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14857) (09-14) splits the harness into five modules evolved separately and evolves on 2,000 tasks disjoint from the evaluation benchmarks; [HarnessPro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26770) (10-19) makes Pareto choices between performance and cost.
- **Evolving the improver itself**: [Meta^n](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24735) † (08-25) fixes the meta-operation and recurses on its input, getting past the roughly two-level meta-depth cap of self-editing systems, and is the only method to score above zero on ARC-AGI-2; [VERSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02616) † (10-02) finds that an optimizer's self-evolution helps only when execution-based verification is available; [Skill^∞](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434) (10-26) writes the skill-optimization procedure itself as a skill and optimizes it with the same procedure, reaching a 60.7% pass rate on 83 SkillsBench tasks against 56.4% for human-curated skills; [Organon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33046) (10-17) lets a scientific-discovery agent revise its own search strategies at runtime and correct its own adaptation procedures; [RSIFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21791) † (10-15) evolves the improvement workflow itself, and [MetaShare](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21887) (10-15) shares failed modifications across lineages.
- **When to move from the harness to training weights**: [Harness Evolution Hits a Ceiling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11655) (10-08) divides the work by failure type, with the harness fixing process failures (blocked calls, loops, exhausted step budgets) and weight training handling content failures (a delivered plan that is poor); [Harness-Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24974) (09-21) distills the behavior a specialized harness induces into the weights and then removes the harness, raising the base model's macro-average success from 23.3% to 44.3%, above the 41.7% it reaches with that harness attached; [HERD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31401) (10-22) learns continually in the harness and then distills the experience into parameters.
- **Model–harness co-evolution**: [HELIX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13951) † (08-14), [CoEvo-RSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078) (10-18), [Self-HPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791) (10-16; Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate rises from 57.8% to 64.8%), [CoHARL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36469) (10-27; one shared policy and an evolving population of harnesses during reinforcement learning, reaching a 27.6% mean resolve rate on SWE-rebench, 6.4 percentage points above harness-only search) and [Training the Whole Agent, Not Just the Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28069) (10-20).
- **Open worlds without ground-truth verifiers**: [Harness²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) † (10-09) guides edits with a contrastive “propose, probe, compose” step; [DarwinX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07545) † (07-31) runs selection over a population of harnesses and admits only variants that extend coverage without regressing.
- **Benchmarks for harness evolution**: [Evo-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09096) † (08-10), [HarnessDev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01437) (09-01; harnesses that models build themselves still clearly trail mature human-engineered harnesses on code, search and research tasks, and the gains from evolution are unstable and transfer only partly to held-out tasks) and [EvoHarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04280) (09-03; expanding the harness by itself can degrade tasks that were already solved).
- **Theory**: [Audit the Scaffold, Not the Checkpoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34924) † (09-28) proposes a dichotomy: when the set of reachable edits is fixed, self-modification must hit diminishing returns, and only rewriting the scaffold to expand that set escapes them; [Self-Evolving Agent Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813) (10-13) uses continual PAC learning to derive generalization and retention bounds; [Understanding the Benefits of Scaffolds through Computational Separations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27157) (10-19) proves that some closures a harness can compute cannot be represented by a fixed-depth forward pass under the conjecture NL⊄TC⁰, so a same-class student distilled from harness traces cannot catch up with the harness; [Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255) (10-27) writes deployed return as the harness's ceiling minus the policy's mismatch, so comparing harnesses with a fixed model mixes the two.
- **Negative and diagnostic results**: [How Do Coding-Agent Harnesses Self-Evolve?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34938) (10-26) had Claude Code review its sessions on 30 SWE-bench Verified issues and propose 125 harness changes, implemented 24 and evaluated 12 of them, and in the 7 comparisons that could be evaluated only 2 changes eliminated the targeted problem; [Does Your Generalist Skill Optimizer Really Incentivize Agentic Tasks?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37201) (10-27) finds that learned skills succeed only 0.1 points more often than the hand-written seed skills on average, that their dev-set gains vanish on the test set, and that they read about 16% more input tokens; [Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858) (10-19) finds that higher plasticity comes with lower stability; [Self-Evolving Agents Can Repair Failures but Struggle to Generalize from Them](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22984) (10-16) finds that experience drawn from failures can fix failures already seen but rarely transfers to new tasks; [Can LLMs Improve Their Own Harness?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30499) (10-21) has frontier models build a harness on ARC-AGI-3 and revise it twice, for a mean held-out gain of only 2.80 points (95% interval -1.44 to 7.35).

### 2.5 Embodied and Physical RSI

In October, RSI papers and lists in the embodied direction appeared in a cluster (the lists are in Section 1.4). The three papers below reach the same conclusion:

- [Toward Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29677) (10-21): existing studies in the physical world concentrate on manipulation and support behavioral improvement, benefits to later learning and procedure improvement, but have yet to isolate gains attributable to recursion.
- [Who Improves the Robot?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18842) † (10-13): proposes an L0–L5 framework that counts only independently verified recursive gain as L5, and concludes that the surveyed literature has not yet demonstrated physical RSI by this definition.
- [What Stops Recursive Self-Improvement in Robotics?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31760) † (09-23): an agentic system ran 123 rounds of improvement on household manipulation tasks; every change passed its tests, yet the target task never succeeded once. The bottleneck was not the agent but what surrounded it: chained perception modules do not understand relations like “the top shelf”, skill chains lock learning onto the first step, the harness decides what the agent learns, and wherever the evaluator is wrong, the agent optimizes toward that error.

In addition, [Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI: Mechanisms, Evidence, and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38266) (10-28) gives an L0–L3 grading and proposes evaluations that compare a reference improver with a revised one from the same starting point; [Where is Physical RSI capability from?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848) (10-19) proposes PhysRSI-eval, a five-axis metric; and [RoboArchitect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42141) (10-30) has a coding model rewrite the harness around a frozen robot policy and, without updating any weights, raises average success in unseen scenes from 9.7% to 43.3%.

### 2.6 The Weight Level: Data and Self-Play

- [Gödel Forest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36675) † (09-29): several agents each grow a search tree of data strategies and co-evolve through a shared memory, beating a single agent by 10.70% on average across six domains of RSIBench-Data.
- [Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12176) † (10-08): for non-verifiable tasks, the model alternates between evolutionary optimization of multi-agent workflows and supervised fine-tuning on self-generated trajectories, acting as both its own optimizer and its own evaluator.
- [R²Orch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22740) (10-15): an inner loop of self-play on self-chosen tasks and an outer loop that improves how the inner loop improves; failures are distilled into skills and skills into parameters, moving toward deployment that needs no skills.
- [Selection Is Not Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22029) (10-15): in self-evolving loops such as R-Zero and Agent0, a task that looks learnable need not yield a useful update; HeirCommit, which decides whether to inherit an update on fresh evaluation questions, cuts the harmful share of accepted updates by 68%–83% relative to random inheritance at the same acceptance rate.
- [Recursive Reward Improvement via Adversarial Meta-Prompt Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21040) (10-15): open-ended tasks have no answer key, so the task-writing model must also write a scoring rubric; an adversary first writes a useless answer that games the rubric, and the rubric is then revised until an honest answer wins.

Late October also brought a batch of papers that apply RSI to a particular modality or domain, such as [ImageRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32120) (10-23), [AudioRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36578) (10-27), [Tempo-Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32433) (10-23), [A-RSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38568) (10-28) and [MoLRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39714) (10-29). Whether each meets the requirements of recursion has to be judged paper by paper with the three questions in Section 3 (our assessment).

### 2.7 Theory and Economics

- **Limits of acceptance gates**: [The Self-Improvement Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21646) † (10-15) proves that in general no terminating acceptance procedure can be both reliable (never accepting a non-improvement) and non-conservative (never missing an available improvement); guarantees can be recovered in the decidable case of bounded evaluation and a finite proposal budget, but a chain of accepted updates can still raise the bounded score every round while the true score falls.
- **Growth dynamics**: [The Economics of Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15802) † (09-14) argues that net acceleration depends on the product of the elasticities around each feedback loop, and a rough calibration suggests today's loops are not yet strong enough for self-sustaining acceleration but are strengthening; [Recursive Criticality of AI Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00137) † (08-31) derives a recursive reproduction number that decides whether improvements amplify or damp; [The Dynamics of Intelligence Explosions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14426) † (08-14) shows that singular growth is harder to reach than economics-inspired models suggest and that the time to go once around the feedback loop is a key parameter; [The Singularity Has a Distribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18445) † (10-13) derives, in a stochastic model, the distribution of the explosion time and limits on detecting it.
- **Policy**: [What if automating AI R&D triggers an intelligence explosion?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36054) † (09-28) assesses the evidence that automating AI R&D could trigger an intelligence explosion and what that would mean, and recommends that policymakers urgently gain visibility into how far AI R&D is automated and study how to steer and constrain a possible intelligence explosion.

## 3. Three Questions for a Paper Named After RSI

In the second half of 2026, many papers carry RSI in their names while actually doing single-round or bounded self-improvement. Section 3.5 of the [previous review](./RSI-Literature-Review_en.md) uses three questions to judge how far a paper is from recursive self-improvement (our assessment):

1. Does what gets changed and kept include the improvement mechanism itself?
2. What stays fixed at the outermost level, such as the evaluator, the selection rule or the base model?
3. Are successive generations of the improver compared from the same starting point at a matched budget?

The lists' grading schemes help answer these questions: lobehub separates task gain, retained gain and improver gain; pinkbubblebubble separates RSI, Self-improvement and Enabler; theseus-labs-rsi grades on an L1–L5 autonomy scale; and in the embodied direction, [Who Improves the Robot?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18842) † counts only independently verified recursive gain as L5.

## 4. Relation to the Previous Review

- **The main conclusions are unchanged.** Bounded self-improvement is abundant and there is no reliable evidence of recursive, compounding improvement; the source of the improvement signal sets the ceiling; the main arena in the second half of 2026 is the harness; measurement is the bottleneck; and the evidence that risk accumulates across generations is firmer than the evidence that capability compounds. The new papers since October 15 all fall within these lines.
- **Three pieces of information are new** (our assessment): embodied RSI has grown from scattered papers into a subfield with several surveys and dedicated lists, whose surveys find that it has not yet been achieved; agents post-training models on their own now have measurable testbeds such as RSI Arena, Athanor and ParetoRSI-Gym; and measurement and auditing papers keep coming, further widening the gap between “evidence was reported” and “the evidence is reliable”.
- Papers the previous review read in full are marked † in this note; their full-text evidence is in [Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) in AI: A Literature Review](./RSI-Literature-Review_en.md).

## 5. Eight Papers to Read First

1. [Auditing the Product, Not the Recursion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27610) †: how much of the evidence for self-improvement is actually reported.
2. [Harness-Delta Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22466): how much of the gain from harness evolution is overfitting and extra compute.
3. [RSI Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27735) †: the safety risks of letting a model train its own successor.
4. [RSI Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33624): how far frontier agents get when they post-train on their own.

6. [The Self-Improvement Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21646) †: the theoretical limits of automated acceptance.

## 6. Method and Limitations

- **Paper search**: through the arXiv API, 8 keyword groups (recursive self-improvement, self-evolution and self-modification, harnesses, AI scientists and automated research, self-rewarding and self-distillation, model collapse and intelligence explosion, skill and memory evolution, Gödel machines and AlphaEvolve, and more) were run over papers submitted to the six categories between 2026-10-15 and 11-02, paging through every result by submission date, which gave 344 papers; adding papers with harness, autoresearch or scaffold in the title and papers that community lists include but the keywords missed brought the total for October 15–30 to 497. For papers from August to October that several lists include, and for the papers cited here, abstracts were also read one by one by arXiv ID.
- **GitHub search**: repositories were searched with several keyword sets and topic tags, and the README, latest commit date, commits in the last 30 days and stars were read for 56 candidates; for the 27 lists devoted to RSI or a neighboring topic, we counted how many lists include each arXiv paper from August 2026 onward. follow-rsi's daily data and AI4AI's `data/papers.json` were downloaded and parsed directly.
- **Abstracts only**: numbers from papers are as reported in their abstracts; this note reproduces no experiments and did not read most papers in full.
- **List counts may be low**: the counts look only at arXiv links in each README, so lists that keep entries in website data are undercounted; most papers submitted on November 1–2 are not yet in any list.
- **Snapshot**: star and commit counts are from 2026-11-02 and will change. The GitHub search is not exhaustive and may miss new repositories with no stars.
