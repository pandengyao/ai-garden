## 4 Historical Development: From Rationale Distillation to Long Chain of Thought

Before long chain of thought (long CoT) distillation took off in 2025, the basic practice of training models on model-generated reasoning traces had already taken shape between October 2022 and July 2024 (our assessment). This section reviews 22 representative works from this period in order of first version (our count), focusing on three design variables: who generates the traces, by what criterion they are retained, and whether traces with wrong answers are discarded or used (our assessment). This section provides a chronological reference for the later sections, which are organized by pipeline stage (our assessment). Foundational works such as STaR and Orca are discussed in later sections (our assessment). Table 4-1 lists the data-construction elements of each work in order of first version (our count).

### 4.1 The Establishment of Teacher Rationale Distillation (October 2022 to June 2023)

Teacher rationale distillation began by moving the answer-based rejection sampling of self-training onto an external teacher (our assessment). Li et al. had GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) greedily generate explanations from 7 human-written examples and used an explanation only when GPT-3 answered correctly, calling this COTE[^96]. The authors list STaR as the work closest to theirs[^96]. Rationalization prompting (RP) instead generates explanations conditioned on the given gold answer, and CROP switches to RP to fill the gap when the COTE explanation is rejected[^96]. In December 2022, three works applied this process to multi-step reasoning almost simultaneously[^92][^93][^94]. Magister et al. used PaLM 540B and GPT-3 175B as teachers, gave the target answer in an 8-shot prompt, and fine-tuned T5 after removing the CoTs with wrong answers[^92]. Fine-tune-CoT generated rationales with zero-shot CoT from text-davinci-002, kept only those with a correct final answer, and could sample multiple rationales per question[^93]. Socratic CoT had GPT-3 175B generate subquestions and their solutions, which were distilled into a problem decomposer and a subproblem solver, respectively[^94]. Fu et al. then used code-davinci-002 to sample 40 solutions for each of the 7K GSM8K training questions, obtaining 130K samples after keeping the correct ones[^95]. In 2023, SCoTD and SCOTT pushed further on the number of rationales per example and on the consistency between rationale and answer, respectively (our assessment). SCoTD sampled 30 rationales per example at temperature 1.0, with OPT models of 125M–1.3B as students[^97]. SCOTT had GPT-neox 20B perform contrastive decoding given the gold answer and generate counterfactual rationales for wrong answers, which were used to train a T5-3B student[^98].

The students in this phase were all no larger than 11B (our assessment). The accuracy of T5 XXL on GSM8K rose from 8.11% to 21.99% (PaLM 540B teacher) or 18.42% (GPT-3 175B teacher)[^92]. A GPT-3 6.7B student reached 33% on MultiArith, while zero-shot CoT on a model of the same size reached only 5%[^93]. After sampling multiple rationales per question, this student improved further by about 26% and 17% on MultiArith and SVAMP, respectively[^93]. In the supervised setting, OPT-1.3B on QuaRel rose from 59.0 with labels only to 83.8, on par with the teacher's 83.3 under greedy decoding[^97]. Budget-matched comparisons that cut each example down to 5 rationales, selected by diversity, teacher likelihood, open-endedness or random sampling, all fell short of the model that used the full 30[^97]. On this basis the authors argue that the number of CoTs is a key driver, and that 50 per example performs about the same as 30[^97]. After specialization by Fu et al., FlanT5-XXL (11B) rose from 16.1 to 27.1 on GSM8K[^95].

Contemporaneous findings disagree on whether teacher CoT is better than the human-written steps that come with a dataset (our assessment). With T5 XXL as the student, Magister et al. found that PaLM teacher CoT scored 21.99 on GSM8K, above the 19.94 of human steps, while the GPT-3 teacher reached only 18.42[^92]. On StrategyQA, human CoT, at 71.98, beat PaLM's 67.15 and GPT-3's 63.77; the authors attribute this to the human set having the most samples, because both teachers answer some questions wrongly[^92]. Socratic CoT trained GPT-2 Large (774M) on GPT-3 CoT for GSM8K and obtained 12.85, below the 14.10 from human steps[^94]. The authors therefore argue that LLM-generated CoT has no advantage when human step-by-step solutions are available[^94]. A filtering ablation by Fine-tune-CoT on Date Understanding shows that, with the same initial number of samples, filtering by answer scores 5–11% higher than stricter human filtering[^93]. When matched on the number of samples after filtering, the human-verified samples instead score 5–8% higher, which the authors interpret as a trade-off between sample quality and quantity[^93]. The variables most likely to explain the disagreement are the teacher's strength relative to the task and the sample size after filtering, not whether the rationales come from humans or from models (our inference, based on [^92][^93][^94]).

Answer filtering does not guarantee correct rationales: on Date Understanding, 27.6% of the teacher samples with correct answers contain reasoning errors[^93]. A preliminary study by SCOTT found that, of 100 GPT-3 rationales, 42% provided no new information beyond the input and 37% did not support the answer[^98]. For a student trained on human rationales, perturbing the rationales changed performance by as little as 1.1% on CSQA, indicating that the student largely ignores the rationales[^98]. Putting the target answer in the prompt raised the CoT accuracy of PaLM 540B on GSM8K from 59.98% to 79.37%[^92]. Based on their analysis, the authors argue that the gain comes mainly from fixing CoTs that were missing a step or had one wrong step[^92]. Rationales generated conditioned on the answer may be post-hoc rationalizations (our assessment). The SCoTD authors also acknowledge that there is no guarantee that the student's outputs actually follow from its rationales[^97]. Specialization also costs general ability: after FlanT5-XXL was specialized on GSM8K distillation data, its BBH CoT accuracy fell from 41.8 to 0.0[^95].

### 4.2 The Self-Training Lineage: From Majority Voting to Expert Iteration (October 2022 to March 2024)

The self-training line has the model generate traces for itself (our assessment). STaR, an early representative of this line, starts from a few examples to infer rationales and learns only from those that end in a correct final answer[^103]. ReST classes STaR among the methods inspired by Expert Iteration (EI), summarizing it as a loop of sampling rationales, filtering by answer correctness, and fine-tuning again[^101]. LMSI, from October 2022, removed the dependence on reference answers[^100]. PaLM-540B sampled 32 paths for each unlabeled training question and determined the answer by majority vote[^100]. All paths leading to the majority answer were kept and, after augmentation into 4 formats, used for self fine-tuning[^100]. In 2023, RAFT and ReST adopted the same template for alignment and machine translation tasks[^101][^102]. In each round, RAFT samples K responses from the current model, keeps the best one according to a reward model, and then does SFT[^102]. ReST splits the process into two offline steps, Grow (the policy samples to expand the data) and Improve (ranking and filtering with a reward model, with the threshold raised step by step)[^101]. The authors regard RAFT as the special case in which each Grow is followed by only one Improve[^101]. In 2024, Quiet-STaR extended STaR to unlabeled web text, having Mistral 7B generate thoughts after every token[^103]. It uses REINFORCE to reinforce the thoughts that help predict the subsequent text[^103]. ReFT first does 1–2 epochs of SFT warm-up and then uses online PPO to sample many reasoning paths for the same training questions, with the reward given directly by the reference answer[^104]. Havrilla et al. compared EI, PPO and return-conditioned RL on the same tasks and models[^113].

Without using any labels, LMSI raised PaLM-540B's GSM8K self-consistency decoding accuracy from 74.4% to 82.1%[^100]. When the same self-generated data were used to fine-tune PaLM-8B, its GSM8K accuracy rose from 5.0 to 33.4, surpassing the untuned 62B model[^100]. RAFT (K=32), starting from LLaMA-7B-SFT, achieved a mean reward of 2.294 on HH-RLHF, versus 2.077 for PPO[^102]. On IWSLT 2014, ReST reached a mean reward of 83.1 after two Grow steps and three Improve steps, versus 70.9 for behavior cloning[^101]. Online PPO, with as much training data as one Grow step, reached 71.6[^101]. After training on OpenWebMath, Quiet-STaR's zero-shot GSM8K accuracy without task fine-tuning rose from 5.9% to 10.9%, and its CommonsenseQA accuracy from 36.3% to 47.2%[^103].

Two controlled studies reach opposite conclusions on whether online RL beats rejection-sampling-style self-training (our assessment). ReFT with CodeLLAMA-7B obtained 53.30 on GSM8K with natural-language CoT, versus 45.10 for offline self-training and 43.59 for SFT[^104]. When Havrilla et al. initialized Llama-2 from SFT on GSM8K, EI's maj@1 was 0.48 (7B) and 0.53 (13B), and sparse-reward PPO's was 0.44 and 0.51[^113]. Their overall conclusion is that the algorithms perform similarly, with EI best in most cases and with a sample complexity similar to PPO's[^113]. ReST likewise found on machine translation that, at equal data volume, multiple Improve steps yield higher reward than online PPO[^101]. The offline baselines of the two reasoning studies differ in strength (our assessment). ReFT's offline self-training samples only from the warm-up checkpoint, drawing 100 per question and randomly taking 10 after keeping the correct ones[^104]. Havrilla et al.'s EI draws 96 per question, filters out wrong and duplicate solutions, and iterates repeatedly until performance on the validation set saturates[^113]. The advantage reported by ReFT may come partly from a weak offline baseline rather than from online sampling itself (our inference, based on [^104][^113]).

Self-training filters can err as well (our assessment). LMSI's majority answers are not necessarily correct; the authors' argument is that wrong answers usually receive support from fewer paths, so the noise introduced is limited[^100]. ReFT exhibits reward hacking on MathQA, which has a limited set of options: CoTs with wrong reasoning that happen to land on the right option are rewarded too[^104]. ReST's reward-model scores keep rising with the number of Grow steps, yet human evaluation rates a single Grow step best[^101]. On IWSLT 2014, online PPO's validation BLEU dropped by almost 8 points, which the authors see as a sign of reward hacking[^101]. Havrilla et al. found that RL did not push pass@n clearly beyond that of light SFT, and on this basis argue that the models do almost no exploration beyond their pretraining and SFT data[^113].

### 4.3 Wrong Traces Enter the Training Signal (October 2023 to June 2024)

Turning Dust into Gold points out that earlier distillation transferred knowledge only from positive samples and discarded synthetic data with wrong answers[^107]. From the second half of 2023, wrong traces entered training in three ways: corrections, negative-sample distillation and preference pairs (our assessment). LEMA first collects wrong reasoning paths from multiple LLMs, then has GPT-4 identify the erroneous step, explain the cause and give a correction, keeping only the corrections whose final answer is correct[^106]. Turning Dust into Gold uses gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4 to sample 8 responses per MATH question, splits them into positive and negative groups by answer, and then uses a dual-LoRA structure to absorb the knowledge on each side separately (NAT)[^107]. Iterative RPO returns to self-training: Llama-2-70B-chat samples 30 responses per question and forms preference pairs with the correct ones as winners and the wrong ones as losers[^105]. The training objective is DPO plus a negative log-likelihood (NLL) term on the winner, iterated for 4 rounds in total[^105]. The authors point out that it differs from STaR in that the latter does only SFT, whereas preference optimization can exploit wrong reasoning chains[^105]. Setlur et al. distinguish three kinds of data: questions and solutions synthesized by GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5 Pro, correct solutions self-sampled by the student (RFT), and negative samples constructed from per-step advantages[^111].

LEMA data brought LLaMA-2-70B to 83.5% on GSM8K, versus 81.4% with CoT data alone[^106]. In a comparison with the same data volume and the same number of training tokens, mixing CoT and correction data still came out ahead on 4 of the 5 base models, the exception being LLaMA-2-7B[^106]. With GPT-3.5 Turbo as the teacher and LLaMA-7b as the student, distillation on positive samples only gives a MATH average accuracy of 5.29, versus 6.81 for NAT[^107]. The questions that two models, trained on positive and on negative samples respectively, answer correctly on the MATH test set show almost no overlap, with an intersection over union of only 0.074[^107]. Iterative RPO provides a controlled comparison on the same set of GSM8K training questions: zero-shot 55.6%, gold SFT 63.5%, one round of STaR 65.2%, the first iteration of Iterative RPO 73.1%, and 81.6% after four iterations[^105]. Setlur et al. found that with SFT only on positive samples generated by strong models, the error rate falls very slowly with the amount of synthetic data $`D`$, about $`D^{-0.15}`$ on GSM8K and about $`D^{-0.05}`$ on MATH[^111]. Their students are DeepSeek-Math-7B and Llama2-7B; self-sampled positives double the effective data volume, and per-step negatives bring a gain equivalent to enlarging the synthetic data 8-fold[^111].

How negative samples enter the objective matters more than whether they are used at all (our inference, based on [^105][^107][^111]). Fine-tuning directly on a mix of positive and negative samples lowered the MATH average accuracy to 3.03, below the 3.88 from fine-tuning on the original training samples, and the authors argue that this introduces undesirable behaviors into the model[^107]. Setlur et al.'s standard DPO pairing of positives and negatives also did not surpass the SFT policy[^111]. On GSM8K, standard DPO without the NLL term reached only 61.8% after one iteration, below Iterative RPO's 73.1%[^105]. The authors observe that without the NLL term the log-probability of the chosen sequences decreases during training, whereas with it the log-probability does not decrease[^105]. The effective practices in preference learning are assigning credit per step or keeping the NLL term on the winner (our assessment, based on [^105][^111]). Self-generated positives have a cost too: Setlur et al. found that training only on self-generated positives amplifies spurious steps, so that performance plateaus or even declines as data volume grows[^111]. These methods still use the final answer as the yardstick (our assessment). Iterative RPO requires reference answers, and on four-option ARC, CoTs that land on the right answer by luck add noise to the preference pairs[^105]. Under QLoRA, only a limited amount of LEMA's correction data can be absorbed[^106].

### 4.4 Scaling Up Corpora and the Transition to Long Chain of Thought (May 2023 to July 2024)

Another line turned teacher generation into large-scale corpora (our assessment). CoT Collection used Codex to generate rationales with 6–8 handwritten examples per group, conditioned on the given gold label[^109]. It supplemented the Flan Collection, which contains only 9 CoT tasks, with 1.84M rationales across 1,060 tasks[^109]. phi-1 targets code rather than reasoning traces, but its practice of "synthesizing textbooks and exercises" is a direct precursor of later reasoning-data synthesis (our assessment). GPT-3.5 wrote Python textbooks under topic and audience constraints, and wrote exercises and solutions under function-name constraints[^112]. Web code, in turn, was screened by a classifier trained on GPT-4 annotations[^112]. Orca 2 shifted the focus from imitation to solution strategies: GPT-4 was prompted, per task, to adopt strategies such as answering directly, reasoning step by step, or explaining first and then answering[^99]. When training the student, these system prompts were replaced with a generic prompt containing no solution guidance[^99]. Orca-Math went further and used agents to synthesize questions: Ask Me Anything expands each seed question into multiple new questions, Suggester and Editor increase the difficulty round by round, and all solutions are generated by GPT4-Turbo[^110]. After SFT, the student samples 4 responses per question, which are labeled positive or negative by having GPT-4 judge whether they agree with the teacher's answer, followed by iterative preference learning with KTO or DPO[^110]. System 2 distillation goes in the opposite direction: Llama-2-70B-chat first answers with multi-step methods such as RaR, S2A, BSM or CoT, and the outputs are screened by majority vote or by consistency under input perturbation[^108]. Only the final answers are then distilled back into the same model, which does not output the intermediate steps[^108].

CoT Collection raised Flan-T5's BBH zero-shot accuracy by an average of 4.34% (3B) and 2.60% (11B)[^109]. Its ablation shows that 10K samples covering 1,060 tasks outperform 180K samples covering only 9 tasks[^109]. phi-1 (1.3B) achieves a pass@1 of 50.6% and 55.5% on HumanEval and MBPP, respectively[^112]. On zero-shot reasoning benchmarks, Orca-2-13B shows a 47.54% relative improvement over LLaMA-2-Chat-13B, which shares its base model, and surpasses LLaMA-2-Chat-70B[^99]. Orca-Math's Mistral-7B reaches a GSM8K pass@1 of 81.50% with SFT only and 86.81% after iterative preference learning[^110]. When the positive samples keep only the teacher's solutions, the results of both DPO and KTO drop by 2.27 percentage points[^110]. System 2 distillation raised Llama-2-70B-chat's accuracy on the last letter concatenation task from 30.0% to 98.0%, higher than the 44.5% of two-step RaR itself[^108]. On the biased and unbiased TriviaQA evaluation sets, the distilled model scores 81.3% and 78.6% while generating 56 tokens on average, whereas S2A itself scores 76.0% and 69.3% and needs 147 tokens[^108].

Orca 2 and phi-1 describe no correctness check on the teacher outputs, and Orca-Math describes filtering against reference answers only for the 6,216 questions from DMath (our assessment, based on [^99][^110][^112]). The Orca 2 authors point out that including prompts from training sets such as GSM8K in training turns the corresponding benchmarks into in-domain evaluations[^99]. Orca-Math's preference labels take the teacher's answer as the standard, so they cannot be corrected when the teacher is wrong (our assessment, based on [^110]). The phi-1 authors acknowledge that the data generated by GPT-3.5 have a high error rate, and that some synthesis details are not disclosed for proprietary reasons[^112]. Codex, which CoT Collection used, has been discontinued, and the authors argue that this is a serious threat to reproducibility[^109]. The failure case of System 2 distillation marks the limit of answer-level compression (our assessment). After CoT on GSM8k was distilled into a model that outputs only the answer, single-sample (k=1) accuracy was only 7.13%, whereas zero-shot CoT itself reached 52.77%[^108]. Its self-supervised targets were obtained by majority vote, 7461 pairs in total, with an accuracy of only 56.81%[^108]. The authors argue that the reasoning required by math problems differs greatly from that of other tasks, and that the method does not always work[^108]. Because the targets themselves are very noisy, the existing experiments cannot tell whether the failure comes from compressing the reasoning or from wrong targets (our inference, based on [^108]). Quiet-STaR's zero-shot gains grow with the number of thought tokens, which is consistent with the later long-CoT idea of trading more reasoning tokens for accuracy (our assessment, based on [^103]). The cost is that several thought tokens must be generated before each token is generated, and the method has been validated only on a 7B model[^103]. The long-CoT data work discussed in later sections (such as DeepSeek-R1, s1 and LIMO) still uses "generate, filter, then do SFT" as its backbone, with the changes mainly in trace length, question difficulty and data volume (our assessment).

### 4.5 Summary (our assessment)

- The basic backbone established in 2022–2024 is "sample multiple traces, filter by final answer or consistency, then fine-tune"; teacher distillation and self-training share this backbone and differ in who generates the traces (based on [^92][^100][^113]).
- The number of samples per question and task coverage are the positive levers with the most consistent early evidence; the gains from the former saturate, and for generalization to unseen tasks the latter matters more (based on [^93][^97][^109]).
- Answer filtering is the cheapest, yet it lets through samples with wrong reasoning and triggers reward hacking on tasks with a limited set of options, which is why process-level rewards are listed as a future direction (based on [^93][^104][^105]).
- Wrong traces must be treated differently from positive samples to provide an extra signal: mixing them directly into SFT lowers accuracy, and standard DPO pairing is no better than SFT (based on [^105][^107][^111]). Rewriting them into correction samples, assigning credit per step, or keeping the winner's NLL term in DPO does bring gains (based on [^105][^106][^111]).
- Whether online RL or expert iteration is better remains unsettled; the existing disagreement likely stems from differences in the offline baselines' sampling strategy, number of retained samples and number of iterations (based on [^101][^104][^113]).
- The strength of the evidence is uneven: most results are before-and-after comparisons against the base model or SFT, controlled studies that vary the teacher, filtering and data volume independently are rare, and several works rely on closed-source teachers that have been discontinued or whose details are not disclosed (based on [^97][^109][^112]).
