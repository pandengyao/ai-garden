## 9 Synthesis of Agentic Trajectories

Beyond the chain of thought, agentic trajectories also contain actions and environment observations, and an action can be a tool call, code execution or a web operation (our assessment). Synthesizing such data requires answering four questions at once: where the tasks come from, how the environment is built, who generates the trajectories, and by what criterion success is determined (our assessment). This section surveys representative pipelines since 2023 by scenario, then discusses recipes and distillation for agentic data, and finally treats the disagreement over success filtering separately (our assessment). For the general methods of question sourcing, generation, verification and selection, see Sections 5–8, and for risks, see Section 10; this section discusses only the special forms they take in multi-turn interaction (our assessment). Table 9-1 summarizes the main pipelines covered in this section (our count, each row based on the papers cited in it).

### 9.1 From ReAct Trajectories to Agent Instruction-Tuning Corpora

The tools of ToolLLM come from RapidAPI Hub; after filtering, 3,451 tools are kept, with 16,464 RESTful APIs in total covering 49 categories[^185]. Both the instructions and the solution paths are generated by ChatGPT[^185]. Since even GPT-4 often fails to find a valid path, the authors replace single-path ReAct with a depth-first search-based decision tree (DFSDT) and keep only the paths that pass, obtaining 126,486 (instruction, path) pairs in total[^185]. With ChatGPT as the annotator, the average pass rate of DFSDT is 63.8, against 35.3 for ReACT[^185]. ToolLLaMA (LLaMA-2 7B), fine-tuned on this data, reaches an average pass rate of 66.7 with DFSDT inference, against 64.8 for the teacher ChatGPT-DFSDT[^185]. Except for the retriever variants, this set of comparisons directly provides the ground-truth API set (an oracle retriever)[^185]. Evaluation uses the ChatGPT-based ToolEval, whose pass-rate judgments agree with human annotations 87.1% of the time[^185]. Under its judging rules, giving up after exhausting the calls without obtaining useful information also counts as a pass[^185].

AgentTuning extends the scope to six types of interactive environments: for tasks that have a training set, the instructions are taken directly from it, while for operating-system and database tasks, instructions are constructed with Self-Instruct and task derivation[^186]. GPT-4 acts as the agent interacting with the environment, and only trajectories with a final reward of r = 1 are kept, except for Mind2Web, where the threshold is relaxed[^186]. Of the 35,341 instructions, only 1,866 trajectories remain in the end, a retention rate of 5.29%, and only 0.52% for Mind2Web[^186]. The trajectories of derived tasks lack thoughts, which GPT-4 fills in[^186]. At the 7B scale, the held-in/held-out scores are 1.34/0.47 with unfiltered trajectories and 1.96/0.65 after filtering[^186]. With agent data only, the held-out score of AgentLM-7B is just 0.09, rising to 0.67 after mixing with ShareGPT at η = 0.2[^186]. The held-out score of AgentLM-70B is 1.40, against 1.49 for GPT-3.5[^186]. But at 7B and 13B, the held-out gain from mixed training is almost the same as from general data alone[^186]. A 10-gram check shows a contamination rate of 47.18% for WebShop, which the authors attribute to the price constraints in the tasks, and their overall conclusion is that no leakage was found[^186].

FireAct has GPT-4 answer HotpotQA training questions with ReAct, CoT and Reflexion, with the help of Google search, and converts all successful trajectories into the ReAct format[^187]. After fine-tuning on 500 successful trajectories, the EM of Llama-2-7B rises from 14.8 with few-shot prompting to 26.2, and that of GPT-3.5 from 31.4 to 39.2[^187]. The conclusions come from only one task and one tool[^187].

Agent-FLAN uses AgentInstruct and ToolBench as its base data, and its main work is reorganizing existing corpora[^188]. Its diagnosis is that existing corpora entangle format following with reasoning, deviate from the pretraining distribution, and can introduce hallucinations[^188]. The authors rewrite the ReAct template as multi-turn dialogue and keep only the samples containing FinalAnswer from the ToolBench portion, for a full set of 24,703 samples[^188]. The 761 queries that gpt-3.5-turbo answered when no tools were provided are used as negative samples, to teach the model to judge when it should not call a tool[^188]. With the same amount of data, Llama2-7B gets an overall score of 41.7, against 38.2 for a reimplemented AgentTuning[^188]. The negative samples reduce the ReAct-format hallucination rate from 15.6 to 9.9, but the Agent-H benchmark used for this was built by the authors themselves[^188].

These works established the procedure that most later pipelines follow: a stronger model executes tasks in the environment, successful trajectories are kept according to the outcome, and the student then undergoes supervised fine-tuning (our assessment, based on [^185][^186][^187]). The chains of thought in them are mostly ReAct thoughts of one sentence per step, and are often filled in or rewritten by another model (our assessment, based on [^186][^188]). Held-in evaluations are drawn from same-distribution splits of the training environments, so their scores should not be taken directly as generalization ability (our assessment, based on [^186]).

### 9.2 Tool-Integrated Reasoning in Math and Code

The trajectories of ToRA consist of alternating reasoning text, programs and execution outputs[^189]. GPT-4 generates trajectories with at most 3 rounds of interaction on the GSM8k and MATH training sets, and only those with correct answers and no tool errors are kept[^189]. In the end, trajectories are obtained for 98.2% of the GSM8k questions and 83.1% of the MATH questions, 16k in total[^189]. The second stage is called output space shaping: the model after imitation learning samples 64 trajectories per question[^189]. Wrong trajectories mostly go wrong only midway, so the authors take their prefixes and have a CodeLLaMA-34B teacher trained on ToRA-Corpus complete them[^189]. After merging, there are 69k training annotations in total[^189]. ToRA-Code-34B reaches 50.8% on MATH, against 42.5% for GPT-4 CoT[^189]. Output space shaping improves GSM8k and MATH by an average of 3.4 and 4.0 percentage points, respectively[^189]. With the reasoning text removed, the MATH score of ToRA-Code-7B drops from 44.6 to 36.8[^189].

ReTool likewise uses trajectories that interleave text and code, but shifts the focus of training to reinforcement learning (our assessment, based on [^189][^190]). The cold-start data is taken from Open-Thoughts and other math data and screened by human experts and DeepSeek-R1[^190]. The authors replace the manual calculations in it with code and its interpreter output, and remove the samples whose answers do not match[^190]. Training then uses PPO, with the policy interacting with a code sandbox over multiple turns, scoring 1 when the answer is equivalent and −1 otherwise[^190]. With Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct as the backbone, ReTool reaches 67.0% on AIME2024 after 400 training steps, whereas a text cold start followed by more than 1000 steps of text reinforcement learning reaches only 40.0%[^190]. A code cold start alone, without reinforcement learning, gives 40.9%[^190]. The external baseline scores are quoted from the literature, and the sizes of the cold-start set and of the reinforcement-learning question set are both unreported (our assessment, based on [^190]).

The two works handle hard questions differently (our assessment). ToRA uses a teacher to complete the second half of the student's wrong trajectories, turning failed samples into valid supervision[^189]. ReTool lets the model explore on its own under execution feedback, with the learning signal coming from outcome rewards[^190]. Filtering by outcome alone concentrates the data on questions the teacher can already solve, so hard questions have to enter training through correction or reinforcement learning (our inference, based on [^189][^190]).

### 9.3 Function Calling and Multi-Turn Tool Data

APIGen cleans executable APIs out of the RapidAPI tool pool of ToolBench, which together with Python functions total 3,673[^192]. Each data point passes in turn through a format check, real execution and a semantic check, and those that pass flow back into the seed pool[^192]. Pass rates differ greatly across generators: 34.42% for DeepSeek-Coder-33B and 84.15% for DeepSeek-V2-Chat[^192]. The authors released about 60,000 data points[^192]. Trained on APIGen data, xLAM-7B ranked 6th with 85.65 on the BFCL leaderboard of June 15, 2024, while GPT-4o-2024-05-13 ranked 11th with 82.94[^192]. When the data removed by the execution and semantic checks is added back into training, BFCL scores drop, and they drop more for small models[^192]. APIGen implements only single-turn calls[^192].

In ToolACE the tools themselves are also synthetic: agents driven by frontier models evolve 26,507 APIs across 390 domains from the API documentation in pretraining corpora[^191]. Dialogues are generated by simulation with three LLM agents, for the user, the assistant and the tools, and the model to be trained assesses sample complexity by its own loss[^191]. Verification has a rule layer and a model layer and requires no actual execution[^191]. In a comparison with 25,000 samples each and the same LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct base model, the overall BFCL scores are 58.19 for ToolACE, 40.51 for xLAM and 24.90 for ToolLLM[^191]. The data with two-layer verification is smaller, yet outperforms data with only rule checks and data with no verification[^191]. The authors also note that sampling by complexity may keep the model in its comfort zone[^191].

APIGen-MT extends function calling to multi-turn human-agent interaction, with tasks taken from the 15 read APIs, 13 write APIs and business policies of the retail and airline domains of τ-bench[^193]. An LLM first generates task blueprints with ground-truth actions, which are checked by a review committee, and trajectories are then generated through dialogue between a simulated user and a gpt-4o agent[^193]. Only trajectories whose final environment state and outputs both match the blueprint are kept, with at most 3 attempts per task[^193]. On τ-bench, the pass@1 of xLAM-2-70b-fc-r is 56.2%, against 52.9% for GPT-4o[^193]. Training also mixed in other function-calling and agent data, and the tasks are taken from the APIs and policies of the evaluation benchmark itself, yet no decontamination check appears in the main text (our assessment, based on [^193]).

TOUCAN selects, from about 2,800 MCP servers, the 495 that are remotely accessible and require no credentials, and has five open-source LLMs generate tasks[^199]. GPT-OSS-120B, Kimi-K2 and Qwen3-32B generate trajectories by actually calling tools under two agent frameworks, and the trajectories then go through rule checks and LLM-judge review[^199]. The full set has 1,527,259 trajectories, and SFT uses only a high-quality subset of 119.3K[^199]. After fine-tuning, the overall BFCL-V3 score of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct rises from 61.73% to 70.45%[^199]. The extension ablation shows that, for Qwen2.5-14B, adding only the core single-turn data lowers τ-bench retail from 44.46% to 36.95%, while adding all the extension and multi-turn data gives 48.48%[^199].

ASTRA starts from 19,036 tool documents of 1,585 MCP servers, samples tool chains by random walks, and then has an LLM synthesize tasks[^14]. Deployed servers are called for real, while servers that exist only as documentation return results from a stateful simulator, with failures injected with a probability of 20%[^14]. Trajectories are evaluated by the mean of seven-dimension automatic scores, with no human annotation at any point, finally yielding 54,885 multi-turn SFT samples[^14]. The reinforcement-learning environments are compiled into sandbox code after question-answer decomposition, and an execution result counts as valid only if it contains the target answer[^14]. The reward is computed as the F1 of subtask completion and call efficiency, and optimizing recall alone makes the number of turns explode until training collapses[^14]. The overall BFCL-MT score of Qwen3-32B rises from 47.88 to 52.13 with SFT, and then to 64.25 with reinforcement learning[^14].

MidTool moves agentic trajectories forward into mid-training[^40]. In its corpus of 20.3B tokens, native agentic trajectories account for 1.8B tokens and 0.42M trajectories[^40]. The native-trajectory branch is generated by GPT-5, GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2 and checked for turn order, schema alignment, required parameters and return consistency, and samples that fail are discarded after retries with feedback[^40]. This part also mixes in rollouts from AWM synthetic environments and filtered Nemotron Agentic trajectories[^40]. After mid-training followed by SFT, Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base outperform the SFT-only baselines on BFCL, τ²-Bench and MCP-Universe[^40].

The trade-off between simulated and real execution is still unsettled (our assessment). ToolACE uses no execution and still leads in the equal-size BFCL comparison[^191]. APIGen and TOUCAN insist on real execution[^192][^199]. According to MidTool, BFCL is particularly suited to testing tool selection, argument construction and hallucination in isolation, whereas τ²-Bench tests multi-step execution and recovery[^40]. From this we infer that simulated returns suffice to teach call formats and tool selection, while multi-turn environment benchmarks depend more on multi-turn interaction data with real observations (our inference, based on [^40][^191]). In TOUCAN, adding only single-turn data lowers the τ-bench retail score, which is consistent with this inference (our inference, based on [^199]).

### 9.4 Software Engineering Environments and Trajectories

In software engineering settings, the bottleneck lies mainly in executable environments (our assessment, based on [^194][^195]). SWE-Gym extracts 64,689 raw tasks from 358 repositories, and after validation only 2,438 tasks from 11 repositories remain[^194]. This step takes about 200 hours of human annotation and 10,000 CPU core-hours, and the selected repositories do not overlap with SWE-Bench[^194]. Rejection sampling with the OpenHands agent yields 491 successful trajectories, while 5,557 remain unresolved[^194]. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-Coder-32B on them raises the SWE-Bench Verified resolve rate by 13.6 percentage points to 20.6%[^194]. Performance has still not saturated at 491 trajectories, and the authors argue that the bottleneck is the sampling budget[^194]. Online self-improvement is limited: the 32B model rises from 19.0% to 19.7% and then stops improving[^194].

R2E-Gym selects code changes from commits, uses existing or generated Fail→Pass tests as the criterion, and back-translates problem descriptions conditioned on the tests[^196]. Of the 8.1K tasks, 4,578 remain after removing those that overlap with SWE-Bench repositories, and Sonnet-3.5-v2 produces 3,321 successful trajectories on them[^196]. The 32B student reaches 34.4% pass@1 on Verified, versus 20.6% with SWE-Gym data[^196]. In a controlled ablation, training with thinking gives 34.4 and training without thinking gives 30.4[^196]. With 400 trajectories each, synthetic descriptions give 27.8 and real issues give 28.0[^196]. A hybrid verifier brings Best@26 to 51.0[^196]. If the execution-free verifier drops the trajectory and looks only at the final patch, Best@26 falls from 42.8% to 37.6%[^196].

SWE-smith injects bugs into runnable repositories, keeps only the patches that make previously passing tests fail, and then has an LM write the issue[^195]. The authors build 50,137 tasks across 128 repositories for about USD 1,360[^195]. The expert agent resolves 6,457 of 17,906 attempts, a resolve rate of 36%[^195]. Easy tasks that are solved again and again hurt the student, so at most 3 trajectories are kept per task, and the final training set consists of 5,016 trajectories generated by Claude 3.7 Sonnet[^195]. SWE-agent-LM-32B reaches a single-attempt pass@1 of 40.2% on Verified[^195]. At the 500-trajectory scale that both sides report (with Claude 3.5 Sonnet as the expert instead), the 32B model reaches 28.2%, a difference of +8.2% and +0.7% relative to SWE-Gym and R2E-Gym, respectively[^195]. R2E-Gym's 51% comes from 26 attempts per instance and is not directly comparable[^195]. Giving the Fail-to-Pass tests in the issue makes the student skip the reproduction step, and performance actually drops[^195].

Both ablations show that model-generated problem descriptions are as effective for training as real issues[^195][^196]. SWE-Gym obtains 2,438 tasks from about 200 hours of human labor, while SWE-smith obtains 50,137 tasks for about USD 1,360[^194][^195]. Teacher resolve rates are generally low, so keeping only successful trajectories biases the training set toward tasks the teacher can solve, and SWE-smith's cap on repeatedly solved tasks indicates that this bias hurts the student (our inference, based on [^194][^195]). Hindsight Hint Distillation, which also belongs to the software engineering setting, is discussed in Section 6.

### 9.5 Synthesis of Web and Deep Research Tasks and Shortcut-Resistant Design

AgentTrek mines web-operation tutorials from RedPajama as task instructions[^198]. A GPT-4o vision-language agent replays the tutorials on real websites in BrowserGym, and a GPT-4o evaluator judges whether each trajectory is correct, with an accuracy of 84.0%[^198]. The replay success rate is 39.9%, and every 1,000 valid trajectories cost about USD 550.75[^198]. In a comparison on 400 tasks, the success rate is 52% when the agent follows the tutorial and 15.78% when it is given only the high-level goal[^198]. The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct scores 22.40 on WebArena, versus 13.10 for GPT-4o[^198]. On Multimodal-Mind2Web, as the data fraction grows from 20% to 100%, the cross-domain step success rate rises from 39.5% to 45.0%, compared with 47.7% for the human-annotated Mind2Web training data[^198].

WebSailor's SailorFog-QA starts from obscure Wikidata entities, builds a graph by random walks over real websites, samples subgraphs to generate QA pairs, and deliberately obfuscates dates, names and quantities[^197]. After open-source reasoning models generate trajectories, the authors discard their verbose native thinking, keep only the successful actions and observations, and have another model reconstruct the thinking for each step in a short-CoT style[^197]. The retention conditions are a correct answer, no more than 32k tokens and more than 5 calls; the cold-start set has only a little over 2k examples, after which DUPO RL is applied[^197]. o4-mini has a pass@1 of 47.3 on SailorFog-QA and 90.2 on the WebDancer training set[^197]. WebSailor-72B scores 12.0 on BrowseComp-en[^197]. The authors acknowledge that some questions do not have a unique answer and that RL was run for only 50 steps[^197]. There is no ablation comparing the reconstructed thinking with native thinking (our assessment, based on [^197]).

OpenResearcher moves the search environment offline: for about 6K MiroVerse QA pairs, a single online search with queries that concatenate the question and the answer first yields 10K gold documents, which are then merged with 15M FineWeb documents[^202]. GPT-OSS-120B, which cannot see the answers, solves the questions with three primitives: search, open and find[^202]. Light filtering yields 97K+ trajectories, and after rejection sampling on answer correctness about 55K are used for SFT[^202]. After fine-tuning, Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B scores 54.8% on BrowseComp-Plus, 34.0 percentage points above the base model[^202]. Without the gold documents, the downstream score drops from 54.81% to 6.35%[^202]. Synthesis issues 5.76M searches in total, which would cost an estimated USD 5,760 to 28,800 at commercial API prices[^202].

QUEST first builds the grading rubric and then derives the question from it: an agent browses the web seeded with trending keywords and organizes verifiable constraints into a rubric tree, and GPT-5 then generates a Python evaluation script for each question[^29]. Strict filtering reduces the objective questions from 17,000 to 5,934[^29]. A manual review of 50 questions finds 2 scripts that cannot be executed and 6 with grading errors, one type of which checks only the literal form of the answer[^29]. Tongyi DeepResearch rolls out each question 5 times; trajectories that meet the bar go into SFT, and the 864 objective questions on which all rollouts fail are left for RL[^29]. Quest-35B, built on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as the base model and trained on 8K tasks in total, scores 80.8% on GAIA, versus 76.4% for GPT-5[^29].

FORT-Searcher points out that structurally complex tasks may still have cheap paths under real retrieval, for example, overly selective clues, multiple clues that share the same evidence, and content that can be searched for directly or supplied by prior knowledge[^33]. The corresponding controls include long-tail entities, multi-source evidence, derived facts and numeric obfuscation[^33]. Drafts are handed to a solver agent that actually solves them, those whose retrieval cost falls below a threshold are rewritten, and rewriting fixes 70% of the shortcut-prone drafts[^33]. In a cumulative ablation on 3000 questions, removing the controls one by one raises solver accuracy from 64.2 to 92.0[^33]. Under the same training setup, the student trained on data from the full configuration scores 61.9 on BrowseComp, while two representative ablated configurations drop to 58.1 and 54.9, respectively[^33]. Raising the average retrieval cost of open-source data from 30.0 to 100.0 lifts BrowseComp from 55.7 to 60.7, while FORT data with the same retrieval cost reaches 63.9[^33]. A manual review of 150 questions finds answer accuracy of no less than 94%[^33]. With SFT alone on 40K successful trajectories generated by DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, and with context management disabled, BrowseComp improves by 5.3 to 18.9 percentage points for students of three sizes[^33].

The focus of deep research data is shifting from making questions hard to closing off shortcuts (our assessment, based on [^33][^197]). Only FORT uses a comparison matched on retrieval cost to show that the training value does not come only from longer searches (our assessment, based on [^33]). OpenResearcher's gold-document ablation shows that difficulty design cannot take effect when environment coverage is insufficient (our inference, based on [^202]).

### 9.6 Recipes and Distillation for Agent Data

OpenThoughts-Agent applies the OpenThoughts ablation methodology to multi-turn agent data[^34]. In each ablation, GLM-4.7-AWQ generates 10,000 trajectories in Daytona sandboxes and Qwen3-8B is trained on them with SFT, for more than 100 controlled experiments in total[^34]. The task source is one of the most important factors: across 95 generation strategies, SWE-Bench Verified-100 differs by up to 30 percentage points[^34]. Mixing the top 4 sources beats using only the top-ranked one, whereas none of the various augmentation interventions on task descriptions beats leaving them as they are[^34]. With GPT-5.3-Codex, the strongest model on benchmarks, as the teacher, the Terminal-Bench 2.0 score is about 5% lower than with GLM-4.7-AWQ[^34]. Removing trajectories with fewer than 5 turns gives the largest gain, still 3.5 percentage points above a random subset on average under an equal budget of about 145M tokens[^34]. The final Qwen3-32B, trained on 100K examples, averages 44.8% across seven agent benchmarks, 3.9 percentage points above Nemotron-Terminal-32B[^34].

The returns to data scale are not consistent (our assessment). Agent-FLAN obtains most of its gains with only 25% of the data[^188]. SWE-Gym has still not saturated at 491 trajectories[^194]. When OpenThoughts-Agent draws additional samples for the same set of tasks, performance plateaus between 31.6K and 100K, but it keeps rising once the 997 base questions of Tezos are expanded through rewriting[^34]. On this basis, the authors argue that the bottleneck lies in the diversity of task descriptions[^34]. Short-format call data saturates quickly, while gains on long-horizon tasks come more from covering more distinct tasks (our inference, based on [^34][^188][^194]).

Agent Distillation compares CoT distillation with agent distillation under the same teacher and the same set of questions[^200]. The agent framework is CodeAct, in which actions are expressed as code and receive execution feedback[^200]. The teacher, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, samples one trajectory per question on 1000 HotPotQA and 2000 MATH questions, leaving about 2,000 after the incorrect ones are removed[^200]. Acting as an agent, the teacher scores 58.4 on hard MATH problems from the training set, below the 71.1 of CoT prompting[^200]. Using the first reasoning step of the CoT as a prefix for the agent's first thought brings this back up to 67.1[^200]. On the average over 8 benchmarks, the 7B student scores 33.54 with CoT distillation, 39.85 with agent distillation, and 42.68 with the prefix plus test-time self-consistent action generation[^200]. However, the 3B and 7B agents still trail CoT distillation on MATH500[^200].

The constraint π-Distill faces is that frontier models in multi-turn environments usually expose only their actions and hide their reasoning[^201]. The authors collect successful trajectories from DeepSeek-chat-v3.1 and turn the actions into privileged information for a teacher that shares weights with the student[^201]. On Qwen3-8B, π-Distill (α = 1) reaches 44.1% on Travel Planner, versus 32.3% for SFT with CoT plus RL[^201]. On τ-Bench retail, the two reach 29.7% and 29.1%, while the teacher DeepSeek V3.1, whose reasoning is visible, reaches 51.3%[^201]. The models in the experiments are no larger than 8B, and Travel Planner's original scoring reward was exploited by the model and had to be modified[^201].

Two further works extend the object of synthesis beyond solution trajectories (our assessment). Unfolding Papers has a teacher model reverse-reconstruct, for 1.8M papers, the writing request, the plan and the thinking that precedes the writing of each section, keeping the body text as in the original, which yields a continued-pretraining corpus of about 60B tokens[^41]. Its writing gains exceed those of continued pretraining on the original text of the same papers, but whether the reconstructed thinking matches the authors' actual train of thought cannot be verified[^41]. Recursive Harness Self-Improvement lets the synthesis harness itself iterate, adopting a modification only if it generates harder yet valid tasks while cost stays under control, and the verifier stays frozen[^53]. After 14 rounds of evolution, the average solver accuracy across three domains drops from 100.0% to 54.8%[^53]. The authors note that isolating the downstream contribution of harness evolution itself would still require token-matched training comparisons on data from the fixed harness and the evolved harness[^53].

### 9.7 The Success-Filtering Debate: Is Keeping Only Correct Trajectories Necessary?

Outcome-based filtering is the default step in most pipelines in this section, but the literature offers opposing evidence on whether it is necessary (our assessment, based on [^186][^194][^202]). The evidence for filtering comes from short-interaction and function-calling settings (our assessment). On 7B, AgentTuning scores 1.96/0.65 held-in/held-out with filtering, versus 1.34/0.47 without filtering[^186]. When APIGen adds the discarded data back into training, its BFCL score drops[^192]. ToolACE's data with dual-layer verification outperforms both data with only rule checks and unverified data[^191].

The opposing evidence comes from long-horizon search (our assessment). OpenResearcher fixes the student, the training recipe and the evaluation protocol and varies only the subset of SFT trajectories: correct trajectories alone give 54.81%, incorrect trajectories alone give 55.06%, and all trajectories give 54.46%[^202]. The authors argue that failed trajectories equally demonstrate search structure, tool ordering, evidence checking and stopping behavior, and they caution against over-interpreting the ranking of the three[^202]. The trajectory filters listed by OpenThoughts-Agent are heuristic rules such as timeouts, subagents and turn counts, and we found no step in its main text that filters by task success or failure (our assessment, based on [^34]). APIGen-MT also acknowledges that the discarded failed trajectories may contain contrastive signal[^193].

At least four variables can explain this disagreement (our inference). The first is that failure means different things: what APIGen removes are calls with format errors, execution failures or semantic mismatches[^192]. OpenResearcher's light filtering has already removed trajectories that are overlong, malformed or reach no conclusion[^202]. If its incorrect trajectories are drawn from the filtered pool, they are well-formed long-horizon searches that go wrong only in the final answer (our inference, based on [^202]). The second is trajectory length: OpenResearcher's failed trajectories call tools 71.7 times on average, versus 38.4 times for successful ones[^202]. OpenThoughts-Agent still finds gains from multi-turn trajectories under an equal token budget[^34]. In long-horizon tasks, the process may carry more supervision than whether the final answer is right or wrong (our inference, based on [^34][^202]). The third is environment coverage and the reliability of the criterion: removing the gold documents drops OpenResearcher's downstream score from 54.81% to 6.35%, far more than the less than 0.6 percentage points caused by correctness filtering (our assessment, based on [^202]). The authors also note that when the corpus lacks the evidence, it is impossible to tell whether a failure stems from a poor strategy or from missing evidence[^202]. When the criterion itself is given by a model, keeping only correct trajectories is noisy as well (our assessment). For instance, ToolEval, used in the ToolLLM evaluation, judges giving up after exhausting the calls as a pass[^185]. The fourth is student capacity and sample size: AgentTuning's filtering comparison is run on 7B, and the filtered AgentInstruct has only 1,866 trajectories[^186]. In APIGen, unverified data hurts small models more[^192]. OpenResearcher's student is Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B[^202]. Extrapolating from the teacher success rate of 56.7%, if no downsampling was done, the three training sets should each differ in size, and the paper does not state whether their sizes were equalized (our inference, based on [^202]).

Accordingly, settings that are short-horizon, structured and have executable criteria should stick with outcome filtering (our assessment, based on [^186][^191][^192]). In long-horizon search, well-formed failed trajectories can be kept, and the budget is better spent on environment coverage and trajectory length (our assessment, based on [^34][^202]).

### 9.8 Summary (our assessment)

- The mainstream paradigm is filtered behavior cloning: a stronger model executes tasks in the environment, trajectories are kept by outcome, and the student is then trained with SFT; the CoT in these trajectories is mostly short per-step thinking and is often filled in or reconstructed by another model (based on [^186][^194][^197]).
- The data bottleneck has shifted from trajectories to tasks and environments: task sources can differ by up to 30 percentage points, model-generated problem descriptions work as well as real issues, and SWE-smith built 50,137 tasks for about USD 1,360 (based on [^34][^195][^196]).
- Success criteria span a spectrum from LLM judges to execution tests and programmatic grading, and every kind of criterion has documented failure modes, including counting giving up as a pass, execution-free verifiers relying on stylistic features, and grading scripts that check only the literal answer (based on [^29][^185][^196]).
- The synthesis of deep research tasks takes shortcut resistance as its core constraint; training value must be demonstrated with comparisons matched on retrieval cost, and environment coverage is a precondition for difficulty design to take effect (based on [^33][^202]).
- Whether to keep only correct trajectories depends on what failure means, trajectory length, environment coverage and student capacity; short-horizon structured tasks should be filtered, while long-horizon search can keep well-formed failed trajectories (based on [^186][^192][^202]).
- Open problems include the following: evidence on RL on top of SFT is still scarce, checks for contamination from training tasks that share a source with evaluation benchmarks are lacking, and the three forms of supervision (native thinking, reconstructed thinking and actions only) lack comparisons under the same conditions (based on [^14][^193][^201]).
