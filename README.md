- [Tracking RSI Research: New Papers from August–October 2026 and a Comparison of GitHub Paper Lists](./notes/self_improvement/RSI-Recent-Papers-and-GitHub-Lists_en.md) · 2026-11-02
  - There are now more than 30 GitHub lists of RSI papers, yet the five largest overlap pairwise by only 0.02–0.15 (Jaccard index), so no single list covers the field: use lobehub/awesome-rsi for a general map, Prism-Shadow/awesome-rsi to search by dimension, pinkbubblebubble/awesome-rsi for the newest papers with an evidence label on each, KaiWU5/Awesome-AI4AI for weekly updates and machine-readable data, and adongwanai/follow-rsi for a daily feed in Chinese. - [Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) in AI: A Literature Review](./notes/self_improvement/RSI-Literature-Review_en.md) · 2026-10-21
  - After a systematic search of 5,784 candidate papers and a full reading of 365, the review finds plenty of bounded self-improvement in all five strands (weights, context/memory/skills, harnesses, AI-designed components and automated research), but reliable evidence of recursive, compounding self-improvement in none. Multi-round gains shrink or saturate, and the outermost layer always keeps a fixed evaluation or selection step; of 90 studies that call themselves RSI, only 22% audit two or more reruns of the full loop. The signal source sets the ceiling: loops anchored in execution or formal verification are the most stable, self-judged loops reverse or collapse within a few rounds, and the evidence that risk accumulates across generations is firmer than the evidence that capability compounds.

### Training

- [LLM Positional Encoding: An Illustrated Guide from Intuition to RoPE](./notes/training/LLM-Positional-Encoding-Illustrated_en.md) · 2026-11-06
  - Eight original figures explain token order, positional injection points, sinusoidal frequencies, relative offsets, RoPE, ALiBi, position interpolation, and the KV cache. Hand-calculable vector and attention examples clarify translation invariance with fixed content and the limits of context extension.

- [Forward KL and Reverse KL in OPD: Intuition, Formulas, and Common Misconceptions](./notes/training/Forward-and-Reverse-KL-in-OPD_en.md) · 2026-11-06
  - Forward KL weights by teacher probabilities to avoid missing teacher-favored choices; Reverse KL weights by student probabilities to avoid overestimating choices the teacher considers unlikely. A three-token example clarifies the distinction and separates KL direction from the source of OPD trajectories.

- [On-Policy Distillation (OPD): New Papers from August to October 2026, and the GitHub Paper Lists](./notes/training/On-Policy-Distillation-Literature-Review_en.md) · 2026-11-02
  - arXiv had 540 core OPD papers in August–October 2026 (128 in August, 212 in October), and another 35 industrial technical reports use OPD. The quarter's through-line is the question of what OPD actually does, and most answers run against intuition: 1–8 prompts come close to full data, supervising only 0.05%–1% of tokens matches full-token training, reverse-KL OPD transfers skills but not facts, and in 14 of 17 teacher-student pairs distilling on offline samples from the initial student works better. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) has the most papers, and more than ten analysis papers question what its privileged information adds; OPD followed by RL beats joint training; multi-teacher OPD has become a common way for industry to merge expert models, yet in a controlled comparison it is no more accurate than SFT merging. On GitHub, the three large lists (chrisliu298, thinkwee, nick7nlp) share only 42 of the last three months' papers, and none has added papers from mid-to-late October yet.

- [Entropy, Cross Entropy, and KL Divergence: Definitions, Formulas, and Their Relationship](./notes/training/Entropy-Cross-Entropy-and-KL-Divergence_en.md) · 2026-10-30
  - Compares the definitions, formulas, and machine-learning roles of information content, entropy, cross entropy, and KL divergence. With a fixed true distribution, minimizing cross entropy is equivalent to minimizing KL divergence.

- [Chain-of-Thought Synthesis for Training Data: Methods, Evidence, and Open Problems (literature review)](./notes/training/CoT-Data-Synthesis-Literature-Review_en.md) · 2026-10-22
  - After a systematic search of 13,589 candidates, 1,527 inclusions and a full reading of 237 sources: for reasoning data used in SFT and distillation, the emphasis has shifted from "find more questions" to "sample many traces per question, then select for the student", and in 2026 it moved quickly toward on-policy distillation (included papers whose abstracts mention it rose from 2 in the second half of 2025 to 79 in the first half of 2026); for code and agentic data the bottleneck is the diversity of tasks and environments. Four intuitions, "stronger teachers are better", "answer-correct traces are better", "more data is better" and "verification always helps", each have counterexamples from controlled experiments; whether they hold depends on student scale, the teacher-student distribution gap and the training budget. Answer-conditioned generation, length penalties and recursive self-training can damage data in ways that answer accuracy does not reveal.

- [Bridged Self-Distillation for On-Policy Learning (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/BSD-Bridged-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-10-19
  - BSD asks the teacher only for a written solution, which is handed as a reference to a frozen copy of the student; this copy runs a per-token check on the student's wrong responses and adjusts how heavy the penalties are, while right or wrong is still decided by the verifier, so teachers with the same tokenizer, a different tokenizer or only a closed API share one training pipeline. In the 12 pairs where 4 Qwen3 students learn from 3 kinds of teachers, BSD beats the untrained student every time, raising average accuracy by 1.2 to 4.7 percentage points, and in 11 pairs it has the highest or tied-highest score among the distillation methods; SFT, which directly imitates the teacher's solutions, gets worse in 8 pairs. Averaged over 6 checkpoints read off the plots, BSD's lead over the specialized methods is only about 0.3 to 1.3 points.

- [Which Skill to Distill? SGUID: Selecting a Compact Skill Bank for Model-Skill Co-Evolution (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/SGUID-Selecting-a-Compact-Skill-Bank_en.md) · 2026-10-08
  - The paper finds that when skills are distilled into a model, most skills retrieved by semantic relevance did nothing: on three Qwen3 models, only 6.4% to 21.2% of skill retrievals actually made it into training. The SGUID method it proposes first runs a trial training pass with all the skills, keeps only a few based on whether each consistently gives a useful signal during training, then retrains from the original model; the improved model then summarizes new skills, which are selected and learned again. On three math competition sets, learning only 6 skills matches or beats learning all 30 to 71 on 3 of the 4 models; after learning 3 more in round 2, all 4 models match or beat it, and Qwen3-8B's average accuracy rises from 62.9% to 66.3%.

- [Dependency-Aware Trajectory Refinement for Efficient Multi-Turn Agent Fine-Tuning (full paper, with a Chinese translation)](./notes/training/Dependency-Aware-Trajectory-Refinement_en.md) · 2026-09-16
  - Treats each multi-turn agent trajectory as a round-level dependency DAG and edits it deterministically on the graph: L1 prunes out-degree-0 dead-end rounds, L2 merges sibling rounds that share both parents and children, L3 requires only shared parents and merges iteratively, and L2a/L3a have an LLM rewrite the merged `<think>` into one coherent passage. With SFT of Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Thinking on 6196 trajectories, L1 reaches 50.04% average accuracy across four multi-modal QA benchmarks, 1.7 pp above vanilla SFT; L2a matches vanilla SFT accuracy while cutting inference tokens from 20.9K to 11.0K. L3 breaks the one-tool-call-per-turn format used at inference and gets stuck 2–3× as often as L2; the Critical-Path baseline, which has an LLM delete rounds outright, falls below vanilla SFT.

- [Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models II: One Training Example (full paper, with a Chinese translation)](./notes/training/One-Shot-On-Policy-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-09-03
  - Pushes OPD's training data down to a single query: on mathematical reasoning, one-shot OPD reaches 68.5 at step 300 (69.8 for full-data OPD), recovering 87% of full-data OPD's gain, and it holds across three model families and four task domains, even on a hard query the student never solves during training. The explanation has two sides: on the data side OPD consumes states rather than queries, so one query already covers 71.5% of the full-data state space while 16 semantically diverse queries cover 98.9% and match full-data training, in multi-teacher OPD too; on the algorithm side the proportion of the remaining teacher–student gap that one update absorbs keeps falling, at the same pace on 1 query as on all 17k, so OPD is data-overfed but algorithm-starved. Even content-free templates and off-domain WildChat queries approach the real-query baseline.

- [Privileged Likelihood Is Not Automatically Value: Three Checks for Token Credit in On-Policy Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/Three-Checks-for-Token-Credit-in-OPSD_en.md) · 2026-08-10
  - A diagnostic study: on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) takes the scores that a model gives tokens after seeing a privileged hint and uses them as credit, and the authors give three checks to run before use: whether the score can tell right from wrong, whether the hint was written by looking at the solution being scored, and how training uses the score. With gpt-oss-20b on AIME 2025, the AUC with which the score tells right from wrong is only 0.505, and the five ways of adding the token score are 30.4 to 40.0 percentage points below GRPO (64.2%), which only checks right or wrong; when SDPO trains Qwen3-8B on biology problems, the AUC is 0.813 to 0.924, and accuracy rises by 26.75 and 28.00 percentage points.

- [Privileged Solutions or Context-Induced Teacher Behavior? Dissecting On-Policy Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/OP2SD-Dissecting-On-Policy-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-08-10
  - On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) has one model act as both teacher and student, with the teacher also seeing the problem's reference solution. This paper replaces that solution in the teacher's view with a different problem and its solution (OP²SD), keeping everything else the same, to separate two roles: "the teacher knows this problem's answer" and "the teacher has a worked example at hand". On Qwen3 1.7B, 4B and 8B and on AIME 2024, AIME 2025 and HMMT 2025, OP²SD scores highest in 8 of the 9 comparisons and is about 10 points above OPSD on 8B; for example, on AIME 2024 the original model gets 28.47%, OPSD 45.00% and OP²SD 55.83%. One fixed problem is enough as the example, and an example with one step made wrong still works; "1+1=2" makes the model collapse, and physics solutions push scores below the original model. The authors conclude that the gains come mainly from a decent worked math solution changing how the teacher scores tokens.

- [Matching Supervision to the Student's Learning Capacity: A Unified Framework for On-Policy Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/USD-Unified-On-Policy-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-08-08
  - On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) has two knobs to tune: which tokens the student focuses on, and how much of the reference solution the teacher sees. The paper argues that the two compete for the same digestive capacity of the student and proposes USD: it puts both into one optimization problem with a difficulty budget, derives a difficulty price λ, and in each batch adjusts both knobs at once depending on whether the average difficulty is above or below the budget. On Qwen3-1.7B, 4B and 8B and three math competition test sets, USD beats plain OPSD by 2.5, 2.3 and 2.1 percentage points, while the variants that tune only one knob gain only 0.4 to 1.8 percentage points; USD clearly beats plain OPSD only when the capacity budget ε is set to 0.3.

- [Privileged, but Biased: How PI-Conditioned Teachers Break Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/PI-Bias-Privileged-but-Biased-Teachers_en.md) · 2026-08-05
  - A diagnostic study: the authors carry self-distillation (the same model, after seeing a reference solution, acts as the teacher for its own question-only self and corrects the student token by token) over unchanged to hard question-answering, math, coding and multi-turn tool-calling tasks, with no reward at all. When Qwen3-8B is trained this way, the loss keeps falling but scores do not rise, and most of them drop: with the SDPO recipe, multi-turn tool calling in think mode drops 3.51 percentage points on average, and with the OPSD recipe, math in think mode drops 4.28 percentage points on average. The cause is that a teacher that has seen the solution recognizes only that one solution: on math its PI bias score for that solution is 0.52, but for another correct solution to the same question it is only about 0.02 or lower; more than half of the loss also goes to function words, punctuation and hesitation words, trial and error is penalized, and in the end the student becomes "flatter" and stops earlier.

- [DAPD: Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/DAPD-Dual-Anchored-Policy-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-08-03
  - OPSD has the copy of the model that sees the reference solution act as teacher for the copy that cannot see the answer at test time, so the student learns to just state an answer when it gets stuck, which the paper calls privilege illusion. DAPD keeps OPSD's original loss, adds a middle role, Self, that "has the full solution in front of it", builds two alignment paths in which both sides have the same information, and lets the reference solution and the student response take turns as the guidance material. On Qwen3-4B, the six-benchmark average score is 2.00 points above OPSD; on five sizes from 1.7B to 32B, the average accuracy on three math competitions is 1.94 to 2.78 points above OPSD; late in training, the number of forced answers, summed over the five sizes, drops from 33 to 9, 73% fewer.

- [Beyond Imitation: Reflective On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLM Reasoning (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/ROSD-Reflective-On-Policy-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-05-27
  - ROSD improves on-policy self-distillation: after getting a problem wrong, the model first reflects on its own mistake against a correct response from the same group, finds the first wrong sentence and writes correction notes; the teacher sees only these notes, not the whole answer, and corrects only from the first wrong sentence on, leaving the correctly reasoned part before it out of the loss. On Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, its five-task average score is first in every case (72.83, 73.45, 53.38), 0.69 to 2.97 points above the enhanced GRPO (using the highest score during training), with no late collapse like SDPO's; in cross-domain tests it is clearly better than SDPO but behind GRPO in all 10 settings.

- [HINT-SD: Targeted Hindsight Self-Distillation for Long-Horizon Agents (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/HINT-SD-Targeted-Hindsight-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-05-18
  - HINT-SD has a long-horizon agent review its whole trajectory itself after a failed task, pick out the one to three steps that really need changing and write one or two sentences of revision advice for each, then, only on those steps, have the self that has read the advice teach the self that has not, token by token; by default it needs no stronger teacher model. After training Qwen3-4B, its average success rates on BFCL v3 and AppWorld are 41.88% and 18.46%, 10.32 and 8.72 percentage points above the strongest baseline on each; compared with OpenClaw-RL, which asks a judge to score every step, the time per training step drops from 84.76 seconds to 37.45 seconds.

- [Learning with Rare Success but Rich Feedback via Reflection-Enhanced Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/RESD-Reflection-Enhanced-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-05-12
  - When successes are rare, SDPO's teacher has only the raw error messages and cannot teach much. RESD has the model write its own analysis after each failure, collects lessons in a mistake notebook shared by all problems, and archives correct responses, putting all of these in the teacher's prompt to correct the student per token. With only 1 response per problem per step, per-task accuracy on Manufactoria-Has rises from under 1% for SDPO to 35.80%, the two physics-simulation tasks reach 4.25% to 7.25%, and FiNER goes beyond the best baseline's 50.25% to 53.66%; it climbs faster than GRPO early in training but is not necessarily better later, and each step takes about 1.5× to 2× as long as for SDPO.

- [The Many Faces of On-Policy Distillation: Pitfalls, Mechanisms, and Fixes (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/Many-Faces-of-On-Policy-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-05-11
  - An empirical analysis of when on-policy distillation (OPD) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) work and why they fail. There are three reasons: the student's half-finished answer leads the teacher astray; the unnormalized Top-K loss adds a "+1" bias to the gradient, and training collapses into a string of "maybe"; OPSD learns only what the cheat sheets of different questions have in common. When Qwen3-14B continues the student's half-finished answers on GPQA-Diamond, its accuracy drops from 62.1% to 46.0%; an RL-tuned 1.7B teacher scores about the same as Qwen3-8B (0.82 vs. 0.83) yet works better. OPSD works on style alignment and system-prompt internalization, and brings no improvement in the math settings tested.

- [Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models: Phenomenology, Mechanism, and Recipe (full paper, with a Chinese translation)](./notes/training/Rethinking-On-Policy-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-04-14
  - A systematic study of what makes on-policy distillation (OPD) succeed or fail and its token-level mechanism. Success needs two things at once: compatible student-teacher thinking patterns, and a teacher that brings knowledge the student has not seen in training — reverse distillation shows same-family 1.5B and 7B teachers are distributionally indistinguishable to the student, and OPD learns thinking patterns rather than benchmark scores. Mechanistically, successful OPD progressively aligns the student's and teacher's high-probability top-k tokens (overlap ratio rising from ~72% to above 91%, carrying 97%–99% of both distributions' probability mass), and optimizing only the overlap tokens suffices. Two recipes recover failing OPD: off-policy cold start and teacher-aligned prompts. It also shows the cost of dense reward — reliability degrades with trajectory depth, failure stems from local optimization geometry rather than signal quality, and support size is not critical as long as Top-1 is avoided.

- [Self-Distillation Zero: Self-Revision Turns Binary Rewards into Dense Supervision (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/Self-Distillation-Zero_en.md) · 2026-04-13
  - SD-Zero has a model play both generator and reviser; the reviser, having seen a response and whether it was right, rereads it token by token, turning a right/wrong reward into per-token supervision, with no need for a stronger teacher or a reference solution. The model first learns to revise responses from about 6000 revisions it wrote itself whose final answer is correct, then distills this skill back into problem solving. On 8 math and coding test sets, the average accuracy of Qwen3-4B-Instruct and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct rises from 49.8% to 60.3% and from 41.1% to 51.5% respectively; by the paper's accounting, at a comparable number of generations SD-Zero scores 4.8 to 10.3 percentage points above SFT, RFT, GRPO and SDFT.

- [Privileged Information Distillation for Language Models (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/Pi-Distill-Privileged-Information-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-02-04
  - The goal is to teach a small model a large model's skill at calling tools over many steps, but many large models hide their chain of thought. The paper puts the large model's successful action records into the prompt as a "cheat sheet" and trains the same model together as the teacher with the cheat sheet and as the student without it (π-Distill); at deployment the student does well without the cheat sheet. On Travel Planner, Qwen3-8B rises from 23.6% to 44.1%, close to the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 (45.0%) and 11.8 percentage points above SFT + RL with the full chain of thought (32.3%); on τ-Bench retail it rises from 3.35% to 31.1%, against 29.1% for that baseline.

- [Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/SDPO-Reinforcement-Learning-via-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-01-28
  - SDPO gives text feedback from the environment, such as error messages, to the same model, which acts as the teacher: it rereads the response it just wrote token by token and points out which token is wrong, then the model moves toward the teacher's view, with no stronger external model and no separately trained reward model. Training Qwen3-8B on LiveCodeBench v6 coding problems, it reaches 48.8% accuracy, 7.6 percentage points above GRPO's 41.2%, and reaches GRPO's final level with only about a quarter of the generations; on science Q&A and tool-use tasks that give only right or wrong, it beats GRPO in 7 of 10 comparisons, with responses only about a third as long as GRPO's; its weak spot is that when the model is too weak, it ends up worse than GRPO.

- [Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/SDFT-Self-Distillation-Continual-Learning_en.md) · 2026-01-27
  - SDFT has the same model play two roles: the teacher also sees an expert demonstration in its prompt, while the student sees only the question and writes its own response; the teacher gives a per-token distribution over the student's response, and the student moves toward the teacher, so with only demonstrations and no reward function, the model can still learn on responses it wrote itself. Learning three new skills separately on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (science Q&A, tool use and medical), its new-task accuracy is 4.0 to 7.4 percentage points above SFT's, and its average score on six old capabilities is only 0.1 to 1.0 points below the original model, while SFT is 5.3 to 12.1 points below; learning new knowledge, it answers 98% of the indirect questions correctly, versus 80% for SFT; learning three skills in a row, it keeps what it has learned. The cost is that training takes about 4× as long as SFT, and on a model as small as 3B it does worse than SFT.

- [Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Language Models (plain-language explainer)](./notes/training/On-Policy-Self-Distillation_en.md) · 2026-01-26
  - OPSD (on-policy self-distillation) has one model play two roles: the teacher also sees a reference solution, while the student sees only the problem and answers on its own; at every position, the teacher gives a next-token distribution that the student moves toward, with no external teacher. With only 100 training steps and only 1 sampled response per problem, Qwen3-1.7B raises its average score on three competition math test sets from 37.1 to 43.4; GRPO trained for 500 steps only reaches 37.7, and in the first 100 steps it generates about 8× as many tokens as OPSD. At 4B and 8B, OPSD beats GRPO by only 0.9 and 0.8 points; SFT lowers the average score at all three sizes.

- [MMAT-1M: A Million-Scale Dataset for Multimodal Agent Tuning](./notes/training/MMAT-1M-Multimodal-Agent-Tuning_en.md) · 2025-07-29
  - GPT-4o adds reasoning, tool calls (image description, OCR, detection, search) and reflection to 5 public visual QA datasets, yielding 902,965 multi-turn dialogues (RR), plus a version compressed to one turn (ORR). After 1 epoch of LoRA fine-tuning on it, InternVL2.5-8B's 8-benchmark average rises from 60.7 to 63.4 and Dyn-VQA from 35.2 to 44.0; applying the format through prompting alone, without fine-tuning, lowers the score. Testing the data also shows that ORR has 16% fewer samples than RR, general reflection only points out "cheating" without re-reasoning, and in many samples the image description already contains the answer; the original image download link is dead, so images have to be found in each source dataset.

- [OpenThoughts: Data Recipes for Reasoning Models (full paper, with a Chinese translation)](./notes/training/OpenThoughts-Data-Recipes-for-Reasoning-Models_en.md) · 2025-06-04
  - Over 1,000 controlled experiments optimize a reasoning SFT data pipeline step by step, yielding OpenThoughts3-1.2M; the OpenThinker3-7B model trained on it reaches 53%, 51% and 54% on AIME 2025, LiveCodeBench 06/24-01/25 and GPQA Diamond. Main findings: having the teacher sample several answers per question grows the data by 16× or more; QwQ-32B, despite lower benchmark scores, is a better teacher than DeepSeek-R1; answer verification and filtering bring no clear gain; using one or two high-quality question sources beats chasing source diversity; and filtering questions by LLM-labeled difficulty or response length works better than pre-training data filters such as fastText and embeddings.
